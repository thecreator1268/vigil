A quiet place to check in. Help that notices when things get harder.

VIGIL lets survivors of atrocities share how they are doing — by tapping, writing or speaking, even with no internet — and lets their counselor know, in plain words, when to reach out.

Built on five promises

  • Safety

    Nothing feels like surveillance. No one is ever shown a number about themselves.

  • Trust

    Before every check-in, one plain sentence says exactly what happens to the answers.

  • Choice

    Every question can be skipped. Tap, write or speak — whichever is easiest today.

  • Collaboration

    Counselors see “what they told us”, never “what we detected about them”.

  • Empowerment

    People can read and erase their own words, and change what they share at any time.

  • Help, always

    Free helplines sit at the top of every screen, never behind a menu.

How it works

  1. 1

    Check in, anywhere

    A four-question check-in on the phone app, by voice call (IVRS) or SMS. It is saved on the phone first, so it works with no signal at all.

  2. 2

    Understood on the device

    Five signals are read locally — the person's own answers, the tone of their words, how their check-in rhythm compares with their own usual, an optional voice sign, and a fixed, auditable list of phrases about safety.

  3. 3

    Synced quietly — or at once

    Ordinary check-ins wait for a connection. Anything that suggests danger takes a separate fast path that retries every fifteen seconds until it gets through.

  4. 4

    A counselor is told why

    Alerts come with a plain-English reason, never a score: “Their last two check-ins were noticeably lower than what is usual for them.”

  5. 5

    Districts see trends, not people

    Administrators see anonymized regional numbers only; any group smaller than five people is hidden.
